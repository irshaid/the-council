# The Council

> Bring a decision. **50 of the sharpest minds in business and technology** argue it out — then the room tells you what to actually do.

The Council turns Claude into a board of advisors. It's not a chatbot with a famous-person costume. It's **50 founders, operators, and investors distilled into how they actually reason** — plus an orchestrator that reads your decision, scores who's genuinely qualified to weigh in, answers in their voices, and lands on **one recommendation you can act on.**

No averaged mush. No horoscope answers. If it's missing a fact that would change the call, it asks you first.

---

## Why this is different

- **50 minds, not 50 bios.** Each advisor is a working *operating manual*: what shaped their judgment, how they decide, their explicit `if X → then Y` decision rules, real case studies of calls they made, the mental models they use, and — bluntly — where each one misleads you.
- **It routes, then commits.** The orchestrator scores all 50 on fit for *your specific* question, surfaces only the 1–4 who belong in the room, and hides the rest. You see *why* each was chosen.
- **It answers, it doesn't philosophise.** Every consultation ends with a verdict — the decision the room would make in your shoes, and the first concrete step. When advisors disagree, it picks a side and says why.
- **It asks before it guesses.** If the right call depends on your runway, timeline, or goals, it asks 1–3 sharp questions first instead of handing back eloquent fog.
- **Business *and* personal.** Every lens also carries a "personal side" — how that person handles conflict, pressure, people, and their own head — so the Council can field "how do I deal with this colleague?" as well as "should I raise prices?"
- **Honest and current.** Each lens documents its own blind spots and failure modes on purpose, flags where a quote is approximate, and is fact-checked for currency (leadership, status, and events verified to 2026). Some lenses are enriched with details pulled from primary long-form interviews.

## What a consultation looks like

You ask:

> *"What's the best way to get my first customer?"*

**1 — The fit table.** All 50 scored; only the ones who belong show up:

| Advisor | Fit | Why |
|---|-----|-----|
| Alex Hormozi | 97% | first customer = offer + leads + sales, dead center of his wheelhouse |
| Paul Graham | 78% | "do things that don't scale" — hand-recruit your first users |
| Sam Altman | 70% | make a few people love it; resourcefulness for the first break |

**2 — The top advisors, in their own voice** — how they read it, the specific move, the blind spot:

> **Hormozi — his move:** Pick one channel (warm outreach for customer #1), build one Grand Slam Offer with a guarantee that removes the risk, and message 100 people before you judge anything. *Blind spot: breaks down for long enterprise sales cycles.*

**3 — The verdict.** What to actually do:

> **Do this:** Today, DM 20 people who already have the problem, with a risk-free offer. Don't build funnels or brand yet — customer #1 comes from conversations, not infrastructure. First step: write the list of 20 names now.

And when the decision hinges on something it doesn't know — your budget, your timeline, what you actually want — it **asks first**, then answers properly.

## What's inside each lens

Every file in [`lenses/`](lenses/) follows the same deep structure — an operating manual, not an encyclopedia entry:

- **Snapshot** — the one-paragraph essence of how they think
- **What shaped their thinking** — the formative experiences behind the judgment
- **How they decide** — their real decision process, plus explicit `if X → then Y` rules
- **Decisions in action** — concrete calls they made and how they made them
- **Frameworks & mental models** — the tools they actually use
- **Strengths to borrow** and **blind spots / failure modes** — where they help, where they mislead
- **The personal side** — how they handle people, conflict, values, and themselves
- **Quotes & references** — sourced, with approximate wording flagged honestly

## Install on Claude

**Claude Code (recommended):**
```bash
git clone https://github.com/irshaid/the-council.git
cd the-council
claude
```
The included [`CLAUDE.md`](CLAUDE.md) turns Claude into the Council orchestrator automatically. Ask any question — routing happens every time.

**Claude.ai / Claude Desktop (Projects):**
1. Create a new Project.
2. Paste [`orchestrator.md`](orchestrator.md) into the Project's custom instructions.
3. Upload the `lenses/` files to the Project knowledge.
4. Ask any question in the Project.

## Consult a single lens manually

Open any file in [`lenses/`](lenses/) — or paste it into any AI assistant — and ask:

> *"Act as this lens. My decision: `<...>`. Give me your read, your move, the one blind spot to watch, and a verdict."*

---

## A note on honesty

Each lens is an **approximation from the public record** — a tool to sharpen your thinking, not the real person, and not gospel. Blind spots are documented on purpose. The point isn't to obey a famous founder; it's to borrow many different ways of seeing the same problem, then decide for yourself.
