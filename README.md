# The Council

> Bring a decision. **50 of the sharpest minds in business and technology** weigh in — then the room tells you what to actually do.

The Council turns Claude into a board of advisors. Not a chatbot in a famous-person costume — **50 founders, operators, and investors distilled into how they actually reason**, plus an orchestrator that reads your decision, scores who is genuinely qualified to answer it, replies in their voices, and lands on **one recommendation you can act on**.

No averaged mush. No horoscope answers. If a fact is missing that would change the call, it asks you first.

---

## Why it's powerful

- **50 minds, not 50 bios.** Each advisor is a working *operating manual* — what shaped their judgment, how they decide, explicit `if X → then Y` rules, real cases they decided, the mental models they use, and where each one misleads you.
- **It routes, then commits.** The orchestrator scores all 50 on fit for *your* question, brings in only the 1–4 who belong, and tells you why each was chosen.
- **It gives an answer.** Every consultation ends with a verdict — the decision the room would make in your shoes and the first concrete step. When advisors clash, it picks a side.
- **It asks before it guesses.** If the call depends on your runway, timeline, or goals, it asks 1–3 sharp questions first instead of hedging.
- **Business *and* personal.** Every lens carries a "personal side" — how that person handles conflict, pressure, and people — so it can field "how do I deal with this colleague?" as well as "should I raise prices?"
- **Honest and current.** Blind spots are documented on purpose, approximate quotes are flagged, and facts are checked for currency (leadership and status verified to 2026).

## What a consultation looks like

You ask:

> *"What's the best way to get my first customer?"*

**1 — The fit table.** All 50 scored; only the ones who belong appear:

| Advisor | Fit | Why |
|--|--|--|
| Alex Hormozi | 97% | first customer = offer + leads + sales — dead center of his wheelhouse |
| Paul Graham | 78% | "do things that don't scale" — hand-recruit your first users |
| Sam Altman | 70% | make a few people love it; resourcefulness for the first break |

**2 — The top advisors, in their own voice** — how they read it, the move, the blind spot:

> **Hormozi — his move:** Pick one channel (warm outreach for customer #1), build one offer with a guarantee that removes the risk, and message 100 people before judging anything. *Blind spot: breaks down for long enterprise sales cycles.*

**3 — The verdict.** What to actually do:

> **Do this:** Today, DM 20 people who already have the problem, with a risk-free offer. Don't build funnels or a brand yet — customer #1 comes from conversations, not infrastructure. First step: write the list of 20 names now.

## What's inside each lens

Every file in [`lenses/`](lenses/) is an operating manual, not an encyclopedia entry:

- **Snapshot** — the one-paragraph essence of how they think
- **What shaped their thinking** — the formative experiences behind the judgment
- **How they decide** — their real process, plus explicit `if X → then Y` rules
- **Decisions in action** — concrete calls they made and how
- **Frameworks & mental models** — the tools they actually use
- **Strengths to borrow** and **blind spots / failure modes**
- **The personal side** — how they handle people, conflict, values, and themselves
- **Quotes & references** — sourced, with approximate wording flagged

## Install on Claude

**Claude Code (recommended):**
```bash
git clone https://github.com/irshaid/the-council.git
cd the-council
claude
```
The included [`CLAUDE.md`](CLAUDE.md) turns Claude into the Council orchestrator automatically. Ask any question — routing happens every time.

**Claude.ai / Claude Desktop (Projects):**
1. Create a new Project.
2. Paste [`orchestrator.md`](orchestrator.md) into the Project's custom instructions.
3. Upload the `lenses/` files to the Project knowledge.
4. Ask any question in the Project.

## Consult a single lens manually

Open any file in [`lenses/`](lenses/) — or paste it into any AI assistant — and ask:

> *"Act as this lens. My decision: `<...>`. Give me your read, your move, the one blind spot to watch, and a verdict."*

## A note on honesty

Each lens is an **approximation from the public record** — a tool to sharpen your thinking, not the real person, and not gospel. Blind spots are documented on purpose. The point isn't to obey a famous founder; it's to borrow many different ways of seeing the same problem, then decide for yourself.
