# Demis Hassabis — Lens

**Who:** Co-founder and CEO of Google DeepMind; chess prodigy and games designer turned neuroscience PhD; 2024 Nobel laureate (Chemistry, for AlphaFold). A lens for long-horizon deep-tech strategy and problem selection.

## How they think
- **Intelligence is the meta-tool** — build it, and you can use it to solve everything else.
- **The mission as a two-line plan:** "Step one, solve intelligence. Step two, use it to solve everything else." Every bet ladders up to one north star.
- **Chess mind → search-and-planning mind.** Strategic pattern recognition, planning over long horizons, reasoning about a huge space of possible futures.
- **Games as the training ground and benchmark** — controlled, measurable environments to prove capability before the real world.
- **Neuroscience-inspired** — the brain is an existence-proof of general intelligence.

## Decision heuristics
- **Research-driven long-term bets over short-term products.** Original ideal: a calm institution "like CERN," not a quarterly race.
- **The problem-selection test:** pick a problem that is simultaneously **(1) scientifically deep / a big search problem, (2) data-rich, and (3) easily quantifiable / benchmarkable.** Go and protein folding both pass all three.
- **Prioritize problems that would otherwise take decades** — discovery and health over gimmicks.
- **Top-down mission + bottom-up science.**
- **Treat blue-sky research as search, not gamble** — "no such thing as failure" if each experiment meaningfully splits the hypothesis space.

## Mental models
- **"Solve intelligence, then use it to solve everything else"** — build the general capability first; applications follow.
- **AI as the ultimate tool for science** — released AlphaFold openly; the fundamental-science layer shouldn't be locked up.
- **The Alpha milestone ladder** — AlphaGo → AlphaZero → AlphaFold: each a rung proving generality on progressively harder problems.
- **Ambition held in deliberate tension with rigor and safety.**

## What they optimize for
- Long-run scientific impact; generality; **measurable, verifiable progress**; problems with civilizational stakes.
- **De-prioritizes:** short-term revenue, product races, gimmicks, speed at the expense of verification.

## Blind spots
- **Commercialization tension** — science-first sits uneasily inside a for-profit race; modest direct financial return relative to output.
- **Safety-vs-speed contradiction he lives** — warns about the race while co-leading a lab that sets its pace.
- **Hard to copy for a solo operator** — relies on long horizons, deep talent, and vast compute. The transferable lesson is the **problem-selection test**, not the resource base.
- **Benchmark bias** — the "must be quantifiable" filter can under-weight problems that lack a clean metric.
