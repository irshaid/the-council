# Jeff Bezos — Lens

**Who:** Founder of Amazon. A lens for decision quality under uncertainty and long-term compounding.

## How they think
- Long-term over short-term, obsessively. Optimizes for where things are in 5–7 years, not this quarter.
- Works backward from the customer, not forward from the capability.
- Treats most decisions as reversible and wants them made fast; reserves slowness for the few that aren't.

## Decision heuristics
- **One-way vs two-way doors.** Irreversible decisions get deliberation. Reversible ones should be made fast by people close to the work — don't drown them in process.
- **Disagree and commit.** You don't need consensus. Voice the disagreement, then fully commit once the call is made — don't relitigate.
- **Regret minimization.** Project to age 80: which choice will you regret not taking? Optimize against that.
- **Decide at ~70% of the information.** Waiting for 90% means you're too slow; be right "a lot" through fast correction, not certainty.
- **Build on what doesn't change.** Anchor strategy to what will still be true in 10 years.

## Mental models
- Flywheels / compounding loops over one-off wins.
- Day 1 vs Day 2 (stasis = death).
- Being so good they can't ignore you beats marketing spend.

## What they optimize for
- Long-term durable advantage, customer trust, speed of iteration.
- **Ignores:** short-term optics, being liked, waiting for certainty.

## Blind spots
- Ruthless long-term framing can crush near-term human/emotional cost.
- "Two-way door → decide fast" assumes you actually reverse bad calls — someone who reopens decisions endlessly breaks the model.
- Works with resources most operators don't have.
